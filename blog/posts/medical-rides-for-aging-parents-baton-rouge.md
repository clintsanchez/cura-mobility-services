## Why rides become the hardest part of care

The appointments are rarely the hard part. Getting there is. Once a parent gives up the keys, the calendar fills with trips that someone has to cover, often in the middle of a workday.

You are far from alone in this. <a href="https://www.aarp.org/pri/topics/ltss/family-caregiving/caregiving-in-the-us-2025/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">AARP and the National Alliance for Caregiving counted 63 million family caregivers in their 2025 report</a>, and seven in ten of them also hold a job. <a href="https://www.aarp.org/caregiving/home-care/transportation-services/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">AARP reports that 79 percent of caregivers provide transportation</a> for an older adult or a person with a disability.

When rides fall through, care slips. A 2020 study in the American Journal of Public Health found that <a href="https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7204444/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">5.8 million people in the United States delayed medical care in 2017 because they did not have transportation</a>. For older adults, the CDC's National Center for Health Statistics reported in 2024 that <a href="https://www.cdc.gov/nchs/products/databriefs/db490.htm" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">4.5 percent of adults 65 and older lacked reliable transportation for daily living</a>.

The need also lasts longer than most families expect. Researchers writing in the Journal of the American Geriatrics Society in 2022 noted that <a href="https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9283277/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">older adults generally outlive safe driving ability by 7 to 10 years</a>. That is years of appointments, so it pays to build a plan instead of solving each trip on the fly.

## Match the ride to what your parent needs

Not every trip needs the same kind of help. The right ride depends on how your parent moves, how they feel after the visit and how much help they need at each end.

<!-- svg:ride-fit | Pick the ride by the help your parent needs, not by the type of appointment. -->

**A family ride or rideshare** works when your parent walks to the car on their own, checks in without help and feels fine afterward. It is simple and familiar, but it depends on someone being free.

**A door-to-door medical ride** fits when your parent needs more than a lift. That means a steady hand from the front door to the seat, help with a walker or wheelchair, or a patient driver after a draining treatment. Our [doctor appointment rides](/services/doctor-appointment-rides/) are built around this kind of help, from the door to the check-in desk. For step-by-step help, read our guide to [getting a parent with a walker or wheelchair to appointments](/caregiver-guides/wheelchair-walker-parent-appointments/).

**An ambulance** is for emergencies and for anyone who needs medical care on the way. If your parent has chest pain, trouble breathing or signs of a stroke, call 911. A ride service is not the right call.

## How to book a medical ride without the stress

A good booking call takes about five minutes when you have the details in front of you. It also heads off most day-of surprises.

Have these ready before you call:

- Your parent's name, pickup address and a phone number that reaches them
- The clinic or hospital name, address and suite or floor
- The appointment time, and how long the visit usually runs
- How your parent moves: on their own, with a cane or walker, or in a wheelchair
- If you or another family member will ride along
- If it is one way, a round trip or a repeating schedule

Book as soon as the appointment is on the calendar. Specialists and imaging centers often book weeks out, so there is no reason to wait. Tell the ride service about mobility needs every time, even if they seem obvious. A driver who knows about the walker plans the pickup differently.

If your parent has the same appointment every week, set up [recurring rides](/services/recurring-rides/) once instead of booking each trip. Standing rides fit dialysis, physical therapy and cancer treatment well, and they free you from the weekly scramble. If your parent is in the hospital now, start with our guide to [planning the ride home after a hospital stay](/caregiver-guides/planning-ride-home-hospital-discharge/).

## What Medicaid and insurance may cover in Louisiana

Cost is often the first question families ask. The answer depends on your parent's coverage, so it is worth a phone call before you assume you will pay out of pocket.

Louisiana Medicaid members are enrolled in Healthy Louisiana health plans, and those plans offer rides to medical visits. One plan, Louisiana Healthcare Connections, <a href="https://www.louisianahealthconnect.com/members/medicaid/benefits-services/transportation-to-appointments.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">offers rides to members who do not have their own transportation and asks members to book at least 48 hours (2 business days) ahead</a>. The same page says it can arrange wheelchair vehicles and hospital or ER discharge pickups.

Each plan runs its own ride line, so check the member card or call your parent's plan to learn the rules and the number. If your parent has Medicare or other coverage, call that plan and ask if rides to appointments are included.

When a covered ride does not fit, such as a same-week booking, a trip outside plan rules or a parent who wants the same driver, a private ride service fills the gap. Ours gives you a clear price before you book. Our [rates page](/rates/) explains how pricing works.

## How to get your parent ready for the ride

A calm pickup starts the night before. A little prep keeps the morning from turning into a rush.

Set out the essentials in one bag: photo ID, insurance cards, a current medication list, glasses, hearing aids and a phone charger. Add a snack and water if the visit could run long or your parent is diabetic.

Choose clothes and shoes that are easy to move in. Closed, flat shoes with a good grip are safer than slippers for the walk to the car.

Ask your parent what worries them about the trip. Some parents dislike riding with a stranger. Others fear holding everyone up. A quick talk about who is driving, when they will arrive and how they will help makes the day easier for everyone.

## Build a simple ride plan the whole family can use

When several people share the driving, a written plan saves a lot of phone calls. It does not need to be fancy. One page, or one shared note, works.

Include these pieces:

1. **Standing appointments:** day, time, place and who covers each one.
2. **Backup:** who steps in when the usual driver cannot, and the ride service number.
3. **Mobility notes:** walker or wheelchair, help needed at the door, any trouble with steps.
4. **Health notes:** conditions the driver should know about, and who to call if something seems wrong.
5. **Contacts:** the doctor's office, the pharmacy and the family members to update after each visit.

Share the plan with your parent, too. Seeing the schedule written down helps many older adults feel more in control, not less. It also makes it easier for you to step back on the weeks you cannot drive.

## What Baton Rouge caregivers ask about medical rides

These are the questions families ask us most often when they start arranging rides for a parent.

### How far ahead should I book a medical ride for my parent?

Book as soon as the appointment is set. Covered rides through a Healthy Louisiana plan often need at least two business days of notice, and private services fill up on busy mornings. For standing appointments like dialysis or therapy, a recurring schedule set up once saves you from booking every week.

### Can I book a ride for my parent if I live out of town?

Yes. Many families arrange rides for a parent from another city. Give the ride service your parent's address, the appointment details and a number that reaches your parent on the day. Ask for a confirmation call so you know the ride is set, and share the plan with a local contact.

### Can I ride along with my parent?

Most door-to-door ride services let a family member or caregiver ride along. Mention it when you book so there is a seat for you. Riding along helps when your parent needs someone to take notes or ask questions during the visit, or feels anxious about a new doctor.

### What if my parent's appointment runs late?

Tell the ride service your best guess for the length of the visit when you book, and ask how they handle delays. Keep the office number handy so the front desk can call when your parent is nearly done. For long treatments, a return pickup at a set time is often easier.

### Is a medical ride the same as an ambulance?

No. A door-to-door medical ride is for people who are stable and need help getting to and from care. An ambulance is for emergencies and for anyone who needs medical care on the way. If your parent has chest pain, trouble breathing or stroke signs, call 911.

### When should my parent stop driving?

There is no single age. Watch for new dents, getting lost on familiar routes, near misses or trouble turning to check traffic. Talk with your parent's doctor, who can assess vision, reaction time and medications. Plan other rides before the keys come off the table so the change feels less like a loss.
